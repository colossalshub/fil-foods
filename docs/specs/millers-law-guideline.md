# Miller's Law (7±2) UI/UX guideline

Source: groupmate's updated user story spreadsheet (Nov 2026), tab "Miller's Law (7±2)". Copied verbatim. This is a design guideline for grouping information, not a requirement and not a limit on how many fields, features, or meals the app has.

**APPLICATION OF MILLER'S LAW (THE MAGICAL NUMBER SEVEN, PLUS OR MINUS TWO)**

UI/UX DESIGN GUIDELINE | Use this principle to organize information into meaningful, easy-to-scan groups. It is a guideline, not a strict limit on the number of fields, features, or choices in F.I.L. Foods.

| No. | System Area | Related User Stories | How Seven ± Two Applies | Suggested Information Groups / UI Arrangement | Important Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | User Dashboard and Home Shortcuts | USR-35, USR-36, USR-37 | Present the most commonly used information and shortcuts in a small number of clearly labeled groups. | Suggested groups: Meal Recommendations; Browse Meals; My Orders; Order Status; Account; Help. Keep the main navigation focused on the primary destinations. | Do not hide important functions just to reach an exact number. Prioritize frequent tasks. |
| 2 | User Profile and Dietary Information | USR-07 to USR-14, USR-38, USR-44 to USR-46 | Break the profile form and profile screen into related sections instead of showing every detail in one long, crowded block. | Suggested groups: Personal Details; Meal Recipient; Health Conditions / Doctor Restrictions; Food Allergies; Dietary Restrictions; Food Preferences; Nutritional Preferences; Guardian / Family Contact. | Keep allergy and medical/dietary restriction details clear and easy to find. Do not combine information in a way that could cause confusion. |
| 3 | Meal Browsing and Meal Details | USR-16, USR-17, USR-18, USR-39 | Group meal choices by familiar categories so users can browse one meaningful set at a time. | Organize by Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner; within each meal time, group or filter the available food categories. On a meal detail view, use separate sections for Description, Ingredients, Nutrition, Portion, and Allergens. | Do not restrict the menu to seven food items. The principle concerns how choices are organized and presented, not how many meals the system may offer. |
| 4 | Order Review and Order Details | USR-20, USR-21, USR-22, USR-23 | Separate order information into distinct sections to make reviewing an order easier before confirmation and payment. | Suggested groups: Order Summary; Selected Meals; Quantity / Instructions; Dietary Notes; Delivery or Pickup Details; Schedule; Payment / Order Status. | Keep information needed to confirm an order visible and understandable. Avoid hiding fees, restrictions, or status information. |
| 5 | Admin Dashboard | ADM-10, ADM-11, ADM-12 | Use clearly labeled cards and sections rather than placing all dashboard data and order controls in one dense screen. | Suggested dashboard cards: Orders; Sales; Customers; Menu Items. Keep order search, filters, order details, status updates, and delivery assignment in their relevant order-management views. | The four dashboard totals are already a manageable group. More detailed controls can appear on a separate screen or panel. |
| 6 | Dietitian Dashboard and Customer Review | DIT-10, DIT-11, DIT-12, DIT-13, DIT-15 | Organize customer information into sections that match the Dietitian's review task. | Suggested groups: Customer Profile; Food Allergies; Health Conditions / Restrictions; Nutritional Preferences; Submitted Meal Selections; Order History. | Allergy, health, and nutrition information must remain sufficiently detailed for safe review. Grouping must not remove or obscure required information. |
| 7 | Kitchen Order Details | KIT-11, KIT-12, KIT-14 | Show a short order summary first, then place preparation-critical dietary details in a clearly labeled section. | Order list: Food; Customer First Name; Order ID. Order detail: Selected Food; Allergies; Nutritional Preferences; Preparation Notes; Order Status. | Do not omit allergies or dietary requirements to reduce the visible item count. These details are important for safe meal preparation. |
| 8 | Delivery Order Details | DEL-11, DEL-12, DEL-13, DEL-15, DEL-16 | Present delivery information in a predictable order, with customer dietary information and destination details separated. | Suggested groups: Order Identification; Food / Items; Customer Name; Allergies and Nutritional Preferences; Delivery Address; Map / Location; Delivery Status. | Show only information needed by the Delivery Staff, while keeping allergy information readable and accessible when required. |
| 9 | Help and Support | USR-26, USR-27, USR-28, ADM-08, DIT-08, KIT-08, DEL-08 | Group help content into a few recognizable topics rather than presenting one long, unorganized list. | Suggested groups: Getting Started; Account Help; Meal and Ordering Help; Orders and Delivery; Frequently Asked Questions; Contact / Support. | Use clear labels and allow users to open a topic for its full instructions. |
| 10 | Navigation and Accessibility | USR-37, USR-48, ADM-01, DIT-01, KIT-01, DEL-01 | Keep navigation labels and role choices clear, consistent, and easy to scan. | Use familiar labels and group related destinations. Keep text readable, buttons distinguishable, and instructions concise. | Miller's Law supports information grouping, while readability, contrast, and accessible controls should also be considered separately. |

## IMPLEMENTATION NOTE

Apply this guideline during screen layout and UI prototyping. The related user story IDs above identify where the interface can benefit from chunking and grouping. This is a design guideline, so the existing functional user stories and acceptance criteria have not been changed. The 5–9 range is approximate; meaningful grouping, task importance, safety, and accessibility take priority.
